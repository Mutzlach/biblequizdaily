About the Fruit of the Spirit Quiz

With 30 questions spanning the names of each fruit, their biblical context, and their contrast with the works of the flesh, this quiz is both educational and spiritually enriching.

Designed for ages 10 and up, this quiz works equally well for personal devotion, Sunday School class, youth group nights, or homeschool Bible curriculum. All questions are based on the King James Version of the Bible.

Understanding the Fruit of the Spirit

In Galatians 5:22-23, Paul contrasts the works of the flesh with the fruit of the Spirit. The distinction is significant: works are produced by human effort, but fruit grows naturally from a living relationship with the Holy Spirit. Just as an apple tree doesn't strain to produce apples, a Spirit-filled believer naturally produces these nine qualities as the Spirit works within.

The nine fruits — love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, and temperance — are not nine separate spiritual gifts to be sought one at a time. They form a unified portrait of Christ's own character being reproduced in believers. Notice that Paul uses the singular 'fruit' rather than 'fruits' — they are an integrated whole.

Each fruit addresses a specific area of human relationships and character. Love is the foundation (1 Corinthians 13). Joy persists independent of circumstances (Philippians 4:4). Peace passes understanding (Philippians 4:7). Longsuffering endures hardship without resentment. Gentleness deals tenderly with others. Goodness acts with moral integrity. Faith maintains trust in God. Meekness exercises power under control. Temperance governs appetites and passions.

Study Tips for the Fruit of the Spirit Quiz

Memorize all 9 fruits in order from Galatians 5:22-23 (KJV): Love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance. Quiz questions often ask for specific fruits by position or ask you to identify which is NOT a fruit of the Spirit.

Understand the works of the flesh (Galatians 5:19-21). Paul lists the contrasting works of the flesh just before the fruit. Knowing both lists helps you answer contrast questions correctly.

Note that Paul uses singular 'fruit' not 'fruits.' This is a common quiz detail — the KJV uses the singular form, emphasizing unity of character rather than separate gifts.

Read the full chapter in context. Galatians 5 addresses Christian freedom — freedom from law, not freedom for sin. The fruit of the Spirit is the positive expression of that freedom as the Spirit guides the believer.
